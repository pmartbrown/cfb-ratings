What the rating means

Every FBS team gets one number: how many points better (or worse) than an average FBS team it would be on a neutral field. If Team A is rated 20.0 and Team B 13.0, the model sees A as about a 7-point favorite on a neutral field. Ranks simply sort teams by that number.

The rating comes from on-field results only. Polls, other published ratings, recruiting rankings and betting lines are not inputs. Last season's games carry a small, fading weight in the first few weeks and count for nothing after that.

What data it uses

  • Final scores of every game involving an FBS team, adjusted for home field, travel, rest and late-game scoring when a game is already decided.
  • Play-by-play efficiency: how often each offense gains enough yards to stay on schedule, yards per play, and turnovers, adjusted for the strength of the opponents faced.
  • Head-to-head results between teams that are close in the rankings: when a lower-ranked team beats a team ranked within 10 spots of it, the winner gets a small bump (about one point at most) and the loser a matching drop.

Data provided by CollegeFootballData.com. Schedules and play-by-play from the sportsdataverse project. This site publishes only the model's output (ranks, ratings, records and movement), not the underlying data.

Résumé Ranking

The Top 30 page has a second list, the Résumé Ranking. The Power Ranking predicts: it says how strong a team is right now. The Résumé Ranking looks back: it asks how hard this team's record would be for a typical top-25 team to match against the same schedule.

  • For every game a team has played, the model works out the chance that a typical top-25 team (the average of our top 25 by power rating) would have won that same game, at the same place, against the same opponent.
  • Opponents are rated by how good they are now, using only games played so far, so an early win is re-priced each week as the opponent's rating settles.
  • Each win adds one minus that chance and each loss subtracts that chance. A win over a weak or FCS team adds about zero, because a top-25 team would have won too. A loss to a weak team costs about one, and a loss as a clear favorite costs a little extra. Added up over the season, the score is the number of wins above what a typical top-25 team would be expected to get.
  • Only wins and losses count. The margin of victory, polls and other rankings are not used. When a team beats an opponent that is ahead of it in score by up to one win, the winner gets a small bump and the loser a matching drop.
  • The match figure is the chance a typical top-25 team would win at least as many of those games. It is shown for reading only; the order follows the score.

An unbeaten team with a soft schedule does not automatically rank first. The Résumé Ranking is published for weeks 1 onward as data/resume-week-N.json.

How movement is computed

Movement compares a team's rank this week with its rank in the previous week's published list. A green ▲ means the team moved up that many spots, a red ▼ means it moved down, and a gray dash means no change. Movement uses every FBS team's previous rank, so a team climbing into the Top 30 shows its real move and "was #N". "NEW" appears only for a team that was not rated the week before. The small number under the arrow is the change in rating, in points. The first week shown has no earlier list to compare with.

"Full", "Scores-only" and "Provisional"

  • Full: the week's ratings include play-by-play efficiency for that week's games.
  • Scores-only: play-by-play for that week was not available yet, so that week's games count through their final scores only. Earlier weeks still use their play-by-play. These weeks may shift slightly once play-by-play arrives.
  • Provisional: the week was built from a backup play-by-play source while the usual source was late. It is replaced when the usual source catches up.

Weeks 4–8 recalculated (Oct 26)

On Oct 26 every published week (4 through 8) was recalculated with the same model and the same cut-off time as its original release. Two things changed:

  • Weeks 7 and 8 are now "Full". They were first published as "Scores-only" because play-by-play had not arrived yet. It has now arrived, but not for every game: week 7 has play-by-play for 58 of 62 games and week 8 for 52 of 56. The missing games still count through their final scores only. Adding the play-by-play moved weeks 7 and 8 much more than weeks 4–6: in each week 113 of 138 teams changed rank (by up to 15 spots), ratings moved by about 0.8 of a point on average, and LSU replaced Miami at #1.
  • Weeks 4–6 shifted slightly. The play-by-play efficiency step now uses a fixed random seed, so the same data always gives the same ratings. Recalculating with it moved weeks 4–6 by small amounts (a tenth of a point or less on average; no team moved more than four spots), and no records changed. The most visible change: in week 6, Notre Dame and Miami swap places at #1 and #2.
